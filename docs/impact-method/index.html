Why we need this

Serious funders pay for proven results, not for activity

Foundations and philanthropists treat a donation like an investment. Before they give, they want to know what each euro will achieve. Today we can't answer that, so the large multi-year grants go to organisations that can.

  • What changed because of you?Today: "31,000 people reached"186 named outcomes: startups created, jobs, pilots, awards
  • How do you know?Today: success stories in a sheetEvery outcome is graded by how well we can prove it
  • Would it have happened anyway?Today: no answerA cautious "351 share" on each outcome, checked by a survey
  • What does my €50k buy?Today: no answerA cost per result, and a clear unit, such as one Startup Weekend in a new region
  • Does my euro go further with you?Today: no answer"Every €1 to 351 unlocks €X" of volunteer time and partner value

Funders compare results per euro

Founders Pledge says the best charities can be 10 to 1,000 times more effective than others, so it checks the evidence before recommending anyone (their method).

We've seen it work

Our PRR grant asked for 100 startups supported. We proved 153, by name, because we kept records.

One method, every funder

The same numbers serve Portuguese foundations, EU calls, individual donors and corporate foundations. Only the headline changes.

The idea

Every outcome gets a score in Founder Steps

A Founder Step is one founder moving one real step forward. Each outcome is scored on three things: how big the step is, how well we can prove it, and how much of it 351 caused.

Where the idea comes from

We copied the approach of Founders Pledge. They publish principles, not spreadsheets, so our numbers are our own version.

  • Count only what you causedOur "351 share" on every outcome
  • Discount what you can't proveEvidence grades A, B and C
  • Know the cost of one result€ per Founder Step, once our costs are in
  • Show how far a euro goesThey say "$1 to us moved $31.10" (2025); we will say "€1 unlocks €X"
  • Use judgement where counting failsChecklists for CLA, policy work and Portugal Tech Week
What counts

We count outcomes, not activity

An outcome is a change in a person's or a startup's path. Events held and people reached are still reported, but they don't enter the score.

Questions

You might be wondering

Why don't our team awards count?

They show 351 is credible, which helps. But funders pay for change in founders' lives, and an organisation scoring its own people looks self-serving. We show them separately, as credibility.

Why is 351's share so low, even for great stories?

Founders have many helpers: incubators, investors, their own hard work. Claiming all the credit is the fastest way to lose a serious funder's trust. A modest number we can defend beats a big one we can't.

Why only 67.6, when we reach 31,000 people a year?

Reach isn't impact. 67.6 counts only outcomes we can name and prove today, so it's a floor. The survey and better story collection will raise it, honestly.

Can I change the weights?

Yes. They are the yellow cells in the Weights tab, and every score updates. If you think a weight is wrong, tell Fernando why, so we change it for everyone.

How do I add a new story?

Add a row at the bottom of the Stories tab and copy the formulas in columns N to R from the row above. Fill in the outcome code, grade and 351's role. If the startup was created at a Weekend, write REC-S1 under "Duplicate of" so it isn't counted twice.

What about Portugal Tech Week, CLA and policy work?

They change the system, not one founder, so they're hard to score. We use a checklist (neglected region? partners co-own it? still alive after 12 months?) and repeat the CLA ecosystem survey every year (Portugal scored 2.98 out of 5 in 2025).
